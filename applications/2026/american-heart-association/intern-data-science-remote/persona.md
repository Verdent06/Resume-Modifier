# Recruiter Persona: Intern, Data Science-Remote at American Heart Association

## Role Summary

This is a Fall 2026 part-time remote data science internship supporting clinical and biomedical research. The intern retrieves, integrates, and analyzes healthcare datasets; helps maintain reproducible data-processing and quality-assurance workflows; evaluates AI/ML and agentic workflows; and translates findings into technical or scientific communication for interdisciplinary research teams. The JD surface is data science / AI-ML, while the company identity is mission-driven healthcare research rather than pure software product engineering.

## Track Decision

- **screen_track:** ai-ml
- **differentiator:** healthcare-research-data
- **track_divergence:** false

The resume should read as an AI/ML and data-systems candidate who can build reliable analytical workflows and communicate technical decisions. Healthcare domain evidence is valuable, but the literal screen is graduate-level data science, Python/R/SQL, reproducible pipelines, ML/AI evaluation, cloud/data tooling, and research collaboration.

## Team & Bar

The reviewer is likely a data science or research-program screener, not a big-tech SWE recruiter. They will scan for the graduate-degree gate first, then for credible Python/R/SQL evidence, ML/LLM workflow depth, data pipeline reliability, and enough scientific/research maturity to work with clinicians, statisticians, and engineers. The bar favors end-to-end analytical workflows, reproducibility, documentation discipline, and careful communication over flashy product UI.

## Screen Criteria

Pass signals:

- Python and SQL appear in context, not only in the skills line.
- ML/AI work shows the whole workflow: data processing, model behavior, evaluation, and operational boundary.
- Data engineering claims include reliability, governance, QA, or reproducibility signals.
- Research-facing work demonstrates careful problem framing and technical communication.
- Cloud, Git, documentation, and testing discipline are visible enough to support collaborative work.

Anti-patterns:

- Undergraduate SWE/product resume that ignores the MS/PhD and biomedical research framing.
- Notebook-style ML with no pipeline, evaluation, or data-quality evidence.
- LLM/agent projects that read as demo apps rather than controlled workflows with safety or evaluation boundaries.
- Skills-list healthcare terms with no actual healthcare, clinical, or research evidence.
- Frontend-heavy bullets that crowd out data science, statistical, and research signals.
