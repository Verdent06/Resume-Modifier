# AI Solutions Intern at The Imagine Group

## Role Summary

The Imagine Group (Imagine Staffing) is placing an AI Solutions Intern with a Western New York compressed-air manufacturer's Sales and Proposal/Tendering team. The intern helps implement AI-driven solutions: developing and optimizing prompts, workflows, and use cases with tools like Microsoft Copilot and ChatGPT; researching emerging AI and recommending practical business applications; testing and validating AI solutions for reliability; supporting pilot projects and measuring outcomes (productivity, proposal quality, time savings); assisting with user training and adoption; and presenting findings to business stakeholders. The JD surface is applied generative AI for business process improvement — tool-and-workflow-first, with Python/JavaScript/SQL and data analysis only "a plus." The company identity is a staffing agency serving a mid-market industrial client, so the differentiating signal is practical, outcome-oriented AI enablement for a sales/proposals function, not core product/systems engineering.

## Track Decision

- **screen_track:** ai-ml (applied generative AI / agentic workflows / prompt engineering / eval)
- **differentiator:** sales-enablement / business-process automation
- **track_divergence:** false

The resume spine should read as applied-GenAI: hands-on work with LLM tooling, prompt/workflow design, agentic pipelines, evaluation/validation of AI outputs, and automation of a real business process with measured outcomes. Because the client is a sales/tendering org inside an industrial manufacturer (not a research lab or a big-tech ML team), the strongest secondary signal is GTM/business-process and delivery-to-stakeholder credibility layered on top of that GenAI spine. This is not a track split — business-process GenAI is squarely within the applied ai-ml bucket — so the single applied-AI track grounds the whole page, with practical business-impact framing preferred over notebook/research framing or low-latency-systems depth.

## Team & Bar

The reader is a staffing recruiter screening resumes against a client req, then a hiring manager on a manufacturer's sales/proposal team. The funnel is resume-forward with no algorithmic OA gate; a human reads the PDF early, so the resume carries real front-end weight. The bar is not FAANG-competitive: they want a currently-enrolled student with genuine, demonstrated interest in AI/automation and business-process improvement, strong problem-solving and analytical skills, excellent written/verbal communication, and the ability to work independently. What wins is evidence of *actually shipping* applied AI — real prompts/workflows/agents, validated outputs, automation that saved time or improved quality — and the ability to translate that into practical business recommendations a non-technical sales team can adopt. Depth in generative-AI platforms, workflow automation, low-code/no-code, and data analysis are the preferred signals.

## Screen Criteria

- Generative-AI platform fluency (Copilot/ChatGPT/LLM APIs) should show through real use — prompts, workflows, agentic pipelines, or evaluation — not as a bare Skills-line mention.
- Workflow/process automation and measurable outcomes (time saved, quality/accuracy lifted, throughput) are the core pass signal; lead with the business problem and land the number.
- Testing/validation of AI outputs (evals, quality gates, faithfulness/accuracy checks) is a strong differentiator for a "test and validate AI solutions" role — surface it if present.
- Data-analysis, Python/JavaScript/SQL, and low-code/no-code are nice-to-haves; demonstrated through a project or role, they strengthen the page but are not floor requirements.
- Stakeholder-facing / GTM / sales-or-proposal-adjacent experience (client delivery, business recommendations, presenting results) reinforces fit with a sales/tendering team.
- Anti-patterns: notebook-only ML with no delivery or business outcome; pure low-latency/systems or frontend depth with no AI-enablement signal in the top half; club-membership or coursework padding without built artifacts; AI tools listed without a described use case; a page that reads as a research resume rather than practical business-application work.
