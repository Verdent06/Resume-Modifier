# Persona — Innovation & AI Engineering Intern (Fall 2026), Delta Air Lines

Recruiter lens shared by the writer and grader. Abstract bar signals only — no candidate entry names, no include/omit table.

## Role Summary

An applied-ML product internship inside Delta's Innovation & AI Engineering group. The intern designs and develops machine-learning and AI-based products that improve customer and employee experience — owning projects from day one, engaging business stakeholders to scope problems, building/modifying models, and presenting results to leaders across business units. JD surface: real ML-model development (neural nets, deep RL, gradient-boosted trees) in Python with TensorFlow/PyTorch or other platforms, plus ML best practices (training/serving, feature engineering, model/feature selection) and literacy across NLP, computer vision, personalization/recommendation, and anomaly detection. Company identity: a legacy enterprise applying ML to real airline customer/employee problems — stakeholder-facing delivery and communication weigh as heavily as modeling.

## Track Decision

- **screen_track:** `ai-ml` (Applied ML / GenAI sub-bucket). The JD's requirements literally test ML-model development, Python + PyTorch/TensorFlow, ML workflow best practices, and applied-ML domain literacy (NLP, CV, personalization, anomaly detection).
- **differentiator:** applied ML for airline customer/employee experience — models shipped against real business problems with stakeholder communication, not research novelty or notebook demos.
- **track_divergence:** false. The company's engineering identity (applied ML for customer/employee experience) aligns with the JD's screen track, so the resume grounds everything in `ai-ml`; there is no second spine to protect.

## Team & Bar

Screener is a recruiter plus an AI/Innovation hiring manager at a Fortune 500 enterprise, not a big-tech OA gauntlet. Funnel is resume screen → HireVue on-demand behavioral → virtual HM interview; the bottleneck is fit + defensible ML depth + communication, and behavioral (STAR) is a heavy filter. Evidence that wins the screen: a real end-to-end ML workflow the candidate can narrate decision-by-decision (data → features → model selection → evaluation → deployment/serving), Python and PyTorch demonstrated through use, and signals of ownership and stakeholder-facing delivery. Prestige/brand buys attention; the screen still rewards substance over keyword density.

## Screen Criteria (abstract)

Pass signals:
- An end-to-end ML project with real modeling depth — training/fine-tuning, evaluation on held-out data, error analysis — not just `model.fit()` or an API wrapper.
- JD languages/frameworks demonstrated in bullets (Python, PyTorch), not asserted in Skills alone.
- Applied-ML domain evidence adjacent to the JD's list: NLP/embeddings, recommendation/personalization, anomaly/consensus detection, or model-serving/inference cost/latency work.
- Ownership + stakeholder/business-facing delivery (shipped to real users, scoped with a client/stakeholder) — mirrors "engage business stakeholders" and "present to leaders."
- Metrics that size impact (accuracy lift, latency, cost reduction), landed as the closer.
- A live GitHub for the flagship ML project.

Anti-patterns:
- ML claimed only in a Skills list with zero bullet evidence (e.g. a framework with no supporting project).
- Off-track lead: hard real-time C++/DSP or pure frontend fronting an applied-ML req.
- Notebook ML with no evaluation, no error analysis, no delivery.
- Thin multi-project breadth instead of one deep, defensible ML system.
- Metric-free bullets that leave the recruiter unable to size the work.
