# Michigan State University — Institute for Cyber-Enabled Research (ICER)

## What They Do
ICER is Michigan State University’s cyberinfrastructure organization: it operates the High Performance Computing Center (HPCC), runs multi-disciplinary research clusters, and provides consulting, training, and documentation so MSU researchers can submit and scale computational work. The unit sits under the broader research enterprise (Office of Research and Innovation) and focuses on enabling computation-heavy science across the university.

## Why This Company
I want to work where shipping software directly lowers friction for researchers on campus. ICER’s mission is practical—helping people get jobs onto shared clusters, understand scheduling and environments, and trust the results—and this technician role is explicitly about building an LLM-backed agent to guide code submission to the HPCC, plus measuring whether that agent actually helps. That matches how I already build: Python services, retrieval- and tool-grounded LLM workflows, automated testing in CI, and presenting tradeoffs with evidence rather than vibes.

## Engineering Culture
ICER is documentation- and training-forward (user guides, getting-started paths for HPCC, support for Linux and scientific software) because the user base spans disciplines, not just CS. The stack mindset is batch jobs, schedulers, shared filesystems, and reproducible pipelines—closer to research computing and platform support than consumer product velocity, with emphasis on correctness, safety on shared systems, and clear communication with non-specialist researchers.

## Relevant Teams / Orgs
**ICER (Institute for Cyber-Enabled Research)** — operates HPCC resources, user support, and the software ecosystem around job submission and researcher onboarding. This role sits with ICER staff to build and evaluate an AI agent for HPCC code submission workflows, manage the implementation repository, and report performance internally and at MSU research forums.
