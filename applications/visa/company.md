# Visa

## What They Do
Visa operates the world's largest retail electronic payments network, connecting consumers, merchants, financial institutions, and governments across 200+ countries. The company processes 83,000+ transaction messages per second and 322+ billion transactions annually, with infrastructure built on 300M+ lines of custom code and one of the world's largest in-memory computing clusters. Visa invests nearly $10B in technology and has used AI in production for 30 years—fraud detection and real-time decisioning are core to how money moves safely at scale.

## Why This Company
I want to work at Visa because payments at this scale is one of the hardest distributed-systems and reliability problems: sub-millisecond decisions, global consistency, and security that has to be perfect. Their backend stack (Java, Python, AWS, SQL, REST) lines up with what I've already built—Spring Boot services, FastAPI pipelines, and AWS deployments—and I'm motivated to see how those patterns apply when latency and throughput are measured in billions of transactions. The Insight Day is a direct pathway into their 2027 internship; I'm aiming to use the deep-dive labs and networking to show I can contribute to backend and payment technology, not just learn about it.

## Engineering Culture
Visa's technology org is built around security, trust, and global scale. They run seven independent data centers with 99.9999% uptime, an in-house AI platform that runs complex models in under 3ms for real-time decisions, and a collaborative structure with regional innovation centers (including London). Engineering work spans backend services, data infrastructure, fraud detection, and API/platform systems. The stack and culture emphasize reliability, observability, and working in regulated, high-stakes environments.

## Relevant Teams / Orgs
The Insight Day is Technology-focused in London; it doesn't name a single team. Roles that align with my background would be backend or platform teams building APIs, transaction processing, or data pipelines—likely Java/Spring or Python services on AWS, with strong SQL and REST API exposure.
