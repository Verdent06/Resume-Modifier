# Mastercard

## What They Do
Mastercard operates one of the world's largest card payment networks, processing over 120 billion transactions annually across 210+ countries. Beyond the familiar card rails, they run a sprawling suite of APIs, fraud intelligence services, and identity verification tools used by banks, merchants, fintechs, and governments. Their core infrastructure — the Mastercard Intelligent Payment Network — handles real-time authorization, clearing, and settlement at global scale with five-nines availability requirements.

## Why This Company
Mastercard's engineering problems are exactly what I want to work on: systems where correctness and sub-millisecond latency aren't aspirational, they're existential. I'm drawn to their fraud and security engineering specifically — building models and rules engines that protect against novel attack patterns on a network processing $9T+ in annual volume is the kind of problem where distributed systems and data engineering intersect in a real-stakes environment. The London office is also central to Mastercard's expansion into open banking and account-to-account payments (via Vocalink, which they acquired), which puts it at the intersection of legacy infrastructure and modern API-first fintech — exactly the kind of technical transition I want to build skills around.

## Engineering Culture
Mastercard engineers at scale operate under strict reliability constraints — their payment authorization network targets sub-100ms p99 latency globally. They've invested heavily in Java-based microservices and distributed systems, with a strong culture around security-first design (PCI-DSS compliance baked into every stack decision). Engineering blog themes include API design for open banking, tokenization infrastructure, and AI/ML for fraud detection. They run the Mastercard Developers portal, which means a meaningful portion of their engineering effort goes into DX: SDKs, sandbox environments, and API documentation tooling. Vocalink (London-headquartered) runs the UK's Faster Payments and BACS networks — making the London engineering hub particularly critical to real-time payment infrastructure.
