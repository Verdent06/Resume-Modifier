# Software Engineering Intern – Summer 2027 at K2 Space Corporation

## Role Summary

A paid, 3-month (12-week minimum) in-person software internship at K2 Space's Torrance HQ, where the intern owns one software engineering project end-to-end and supports live on-orbit operations. The work touches real-time, fault-tolerant control software for a 20kW+ spacecraft: subsystem applications across propulsion, guidance/attitude control, thermal, power, and communications; state machines for robust operation; tooling that lets quality code deploy fast; data analysis and anomaly investigation on software bugs; subsystem integration and test support; and dev/test infrastructure that raises reliability. The literal screen surface is generic SWE: currently enrolled in a Bachelor's/Master's in CS, CE, EE, or a related engineering discipline; 6+ months of technical engineering experience *outside* the classroom (lab, research, project teams, personal projects, or prior internship/work); and development experience in Rust, C/C++, or Go. Nice-to-haves are unmistakably systems: spacecraft flight software, Linux systems programming and driver development, CI/CD, hard real-time embedded work (bare-metal or RTOS), electronics/computer architecture/control-systems basics, and HITL/HOTL test rigs. The company identity is mass-produced high-power satellites — mission-critical flight software on real hardware, not consumer web.

Aerospace domain experience is explicitly not a requirement — claimed spacecraft work is not the signal; transferable mission-critical rigor is.

## Team & Bar

The reviewer is a hiring engineer at a ~300-person, Series-D satellite manufacturer screening a Greenhouse pile for a single intern seat, with resume plus technical rounds as the bottleneck and roughly a 5–10% funnel **[directional]**. There is no standard published intern OA **[directional]**, so the page carries more front-end weight than at an OA-gated big-tech pipeline (`recruiting.md`: startups/mid-size on Greenhouse get a human read early), and the technical round tests practical coding and systems reasoning rather than puzzle volume.

Evidence that wins: one of the JD's named languages demonstrated *inside* bullets rather than parked in a Skills line; software that shipped and that other people depended on, satisfying the literal 6-month out-of-classroom bar; real-time or resource-constrained engineering with the constraint named (allocation, locking, latency, throughput, thread boundaries); debugging and anomaly investigation with a before/after number, since the role explicitly owns bug and anomaly work; state-machine, control, or lifecycle reasoning about a system that must not misbehave; build/test/CI infrastructure ownership; and hardware-, sensor-, or signal-adjacent work over pure CRUD. Impact metrics must size a real outcome — latency, failure-rate reduction, throughput, cost, scale — not count artifacts. Availability and eligibility are read as gates, not accomplishments: enrollment, ≥12 weeks onsite in Torrance, U.S. Person status.

## Screen Criteria

- At least one JD language (Rust, C/C++, Go) proven through use in bullets, with the engineering decision that justified it — a Skills-line mention alone reads as a floor miss at a company whose flight code is written in that class of language.
- Real-time / fault-tolerant / resource-constrained systems evidence visible in the top half of the page, with the constraint stated explicitly rather than implied.
- General SWE delivery breadth backing the literal screen: services or APIs, data handling, deployment or containerization, and automated build/test — end-to-end ownership of something used by someone other than the author.
- Debugging, anomaly investigation, or failure-recovery work told as a diagnosis with an outcome, not as a maintenance chore.
- Out-of-classroom engineering that clearly exceeds six months and is legible as such from dates and scope.
- Impact metrics that size outcomes (latency, failure rate, throughput, cost, hours eliminated, revenue) rather than vanity counts.
- Anti-patterns: a page that reads purely web/CRUD or purely agentic-LLM product work at a spacecraft-software company; the required language present only in Skills; thin single-bullet entries that underuse a real role; club or membership padding without engineering depth; notebook ML with no delivery; claimed flight-software or spacecraft domain work the bullets cannot substantiate; keyword stuffing of systems terms (RTOS, bare-metal, drivers) with no backing bullet.
