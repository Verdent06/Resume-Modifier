# Framer Front-End Intern at de Anda Capital

## Role Summary

This is a remote internship for a small family office / independent sponsor that needs client-facing pages, blog layouts, landing pages, visual assets, and reusable visual systems in Framer. The JD's surface is design-forward frontend execution rather than traditional software engineering: the intern is expected to take written content or a brief and turn it into polished, conversion-oriented pages. The company identity is operator-led finance/acquisition work where marketing pages must make trust, clarity, and deal flow easier.

## Track Decision

- **screen_track:** full-stack
- **differentiator:** design-forward frontend / AI-assisted web publishing
- **track_divergence:** true

The screen tests frontend/product presentation evidence: shipped pages, visual hierarchy, HTML/CSS fundamentals, UI components, and AI-assisted iteration. The differentiator is not backend scale; it is whether the candidate can make polished, user-facing pages that a small team would confidently publish.

## Team & Bar

The first reader is likely a founder, partner, or lean-team operator rather than a large-company technical recruiter. They will scan for proof that the candidate has built real things, can own a page or section from brief to publish, and communicates with low overhead. The bar is practical: client-facing polish, web fundamentals, clean organization, and enough autonomy to turn feedback into a better page quickly.

## Screen Criteria

- Shipped UI/product work should appear high on the page; pure backend, infra, robotics, or research depth is secondary unless it supports a visible product.
- Frontend technologies must be demonstrated in bullets, not only listed in Skills. React/Next.js or similar can partially substitute for web-building depth, but the JD's strongest filter is Framer plus HTML/CSS.
- Visual-system evidence matters: layout, hierarchy, typography, graphics, components, or client-facing polish should be legible in the top half.
- AI-tool fluency is a positive signal only when tied to shipping or iteration, not as a generic interest.
- Anti-patterns: a resume that reads like backend/AI infrastructure only; no visible frontend ownership; no portfolio/site signal; or a skills list that claims web basics without page-building proof.
