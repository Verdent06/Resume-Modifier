# Software Engineer, Intern (Summer 2027) at Pylon

## Role Summary

A Summer 2027 onsite internship at a high-growth, AI-native B2B support startup (YC W23, a16z/Bain-backed, ~120 people, ~$10M ARR). The intern builds product features that let post-sales teams (Customer Support & Customer Success) run more efficiently by leveraging AI: prototyping, working independently, iterating quickly, and shipping fast. The role emphasizes high autonomy, end-to-end ownership, and self-directed project management in a highly collaborative, non-waterfall team alongside senior engineers from Samsara, Affinity, Airbnb, and Meta. The JD surface is a general full-stack SWE intern (pursuing a CS/engineering degree, graduating Dec 2026–Summer 2028, in SF or willing to relocate). The company identity is AI-native product engineering — agentic support workflows over omnichannel B2B conversations — with a React / Go / GraphQL / AWS stack named as a *bonus*, not a filter.

## Track Decision

- **screen_track:** full-stack (general SWE requirements — build product features end-to-end, prototype, ship fast, own work; JD names no gating language, only bonus stack familiarity)
- **differentiator:** AI-native / agentic B2B product engineering — building shippable product features that leverage AI/LLM workflows, with startup ambiguity-navigation and ship-fast autonomy
- **track_divergence:** false

The screen spine and the company identity agree. `reference/resume.md` Part III §12 governs: lead with a genuinely engineered, shippable full-stack product (frontend framework, backend service/API, a datastore, cloud deploy, ideally a CI step) narrated decision-by-decision, with breadth across the stack and depth in at least one layer. The differentiator does not pull toward a different track (not an ML-research or infra role) — it sharpens emphasis *within* full-stack toward AI-native product building: an agentic/LLM pipeline wired into a shipped product with metrics, plus evidence of shipping fast and owning work end-to-end under startup ambiguity. The single full-stack track grounds ordering, lead entry, and skills.

## Team & Bar

Pylon is an ~120-person, YC-W23, a16z/Bain-backed AI-native SaaS scale-up growing 5x+ YoY — a human reads the PDF early (Ashby ATS), so resume signal carries real front-end weight, unlike an OA-gated big-tech pipeline. The recruiter/hiring-manager voice: an intern who already builds and ships real product features, moves fast, thrives with high autonomy in an ambiguous fast-growth environment, and treats AI as a leveraged part of the build loop — not a tutorial-repo hobbyist and not an algorithm-only competitor. The binding evidence is a defensible, shipped full-stack product plus demonstrated AI-native building; tech-stack overlap (React, Go, GraphQL, AWS) is a bonus that buys a few seconds, not a gate. Prior internship experience or side projects building product features are explicitly rewarded, as is prior high-growth-startup experience and the ability to juggle multiple workstreams. Onsite San Francisco (willing to relocate); enrollment and the Dec 2026–Summer 2028 graduation window are the only hard eligibility gates.

## Screen Criteria

- Shipped, defensible full-stack product built end-to-end (spec → build → ship) for real users — an app or API with a frontend framework, a backend service/API, a datastore, and a cloud/deploy path, with trade-offs the candidate can narrate; not a tutorial clone.
- AI-native building demonstrated through a shipped pipeline or product feature with metrics (agentic/LLM workflow, eval discipline) — reads as "leverages AI to ship product," matching the JD's "build features … leveraging AI" and "highly leverage AI for software development" signals; buzzwords without architecture or delivery do not count.
- Evidence of shipping fast, high autonomy, and end-to-end ownership — a founded/owned product, a sole-engineer delivery, or a self-directed build that shows the candidate can own and project-manage their own work under ambiguity.
- Startup / product-building signal — side projects or internships that built actual product features, ideally in a fast-moving or ambiguous environment.
- A dynamic-language / frontend-framework floor demonstrated inside bullets (React/TypeScript, Python), not asserted in a Skills line alone — the stack is React/Go, so shipped web-product depth matters more than any single language.
- Clickable GitHub / public-code footprint a recruiter can open — live repos beat a skills list for an engineering-craft startup.
- Class-year on page shows current enrollment satisfying the Dec 2026–Summer 2028 window (`Expected May 2028` clears it; rising senior returning to school after Summer 2027).

## Anti-Patterns

- A full-stack spine that is all backend/data pipelines with no shipped, user-facing product a recruiter can picture as a "feature."
- AI/agentic/LLM buzzwords with no pipeline architecture, no metrics, and no delivery — reads as hype, not building.
- Pure-quant/GTM or pure-data-engineering framing as the lead with no product-feature or full-stack shipping signal.
- Deep systems/off-axis project work (e.g. low-level C++/audio DSP) occupying a prime slot without reinforcing the full-stack product + AI-native spine.
- Thin single-bullet filler entries that dilute a strong shipped-product + AI-native spine.
- No visible GitHub/public-code footprint for a craft-first startup.
- Notebook ML or research-only framing with no product delivery (this is a product-engineering role, not an ML-research role).
