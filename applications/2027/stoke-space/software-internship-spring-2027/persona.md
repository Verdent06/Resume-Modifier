# Spring 2027 Internship - Software at Stoke Space

## Role Summary

A Spring 2027 (Jan–April), onsite software internship at a fully-reusable-rocket company in Kent, WA. The intern owns a real, open-ended chunk of software end to end — design, implementation, testing, deployment — under a fast, self-directed, "learn on the fly" culture. Three sub-teams hire: **Boltline** (web-based hardware-manufacturing SaaS, JS/TS full-stack), **Vehicle Software** (real-time control across bench/environmental testers, engine stands, and flight systems; Rust/C/C++), and **Data** (real-time + post-launch telemetry into analysis, Rust + full-stack). The general bar is project-level command of Python, C, or Rust, exceptional engineering fundamentals, and proof you can drive a complex technical project through execution. The JD surface (Boltline) is general/full-stack SWE; the company identity is mission-critical, real-time aerospace systems.

## Track Decision

- **screen_track:** full-stack
- **differentiator:** aerospace / real-time, mission-critical systems on real hardware (the vehicle-software and test-stand spine, hardware-software boundary)
- **track_divergence:** true

The literal screen for the candidate's strongest path (Boltline) tests JS/TS full-stack and front-end/back-end delivery, plus the general Python/C/Rust + fundamentals bar — so the resume spine is full-stack/general SWE. But Stoke is a rocket company: the memorable, company-fit signal is software that runs on real hardware under real-time, operational constraints. The resume leads with the full-stack spine and keeps real-time / hardware-software / control-loop evidence prominent and deep — it does not collapse into a pure embedded or pure web resume.

## Team & Bar

The reviewer is screening a small, competitive aerospace-startup intern cohort on Greenhouse, with a team-preference question routing to Boltline, Vehicle Software, or Data. They reward evidence that the candidate ships and owns software others depend on, can self-direct through ambiguity, and has driven a hard technical project to a working result. Strong signals: shipped, production-style systems with clear ownership; project-level fluency in a real language demonstrated inside bullets; engineering-fundamentals depth (concurrency, latency, failure handling); real-time or hardware-adjacent work; and metrics that size real outcomes (latency, throughput, control-loop rate, recovery). Brand-name polish matters less than urgency, defensible depth, and the ability to take something from design to running.

## Screen Criteria

- Demonstrates a JD language through use — Python and/or full-stack JS/TS in bullets, not only in a skills line; C/C++ depth reads as a bonus toward the vehicle/systems identity.
- Shows full-stack delivery for the Boltline path: front-end framework work, back-end/API ownership, a database, and cloud/deployment.
- Keeps at least one real-time / hardware / control-loop / mission-critical entry visible in the top half (the company-fit differentiator) without turning the page into a narrow specialist resume.
- Uses impact metrics (latency, throughput, control-loop frequency, command latency, failure recovery, scale) rather than vanity counts.
- Signals self-direction and end-to-end ownership: a complex project taken from design through deployment, root-caused failures, operational discipline.
- Anti-patterns: club-ops or membership filler without engineering depth; skills-list keyword stuffing with no backing bullet; ML/robotics claims that read as demos rather than engineered, owned systems; a resume that reads purely web with zero systems/hardware adjacency at a rocket company.
