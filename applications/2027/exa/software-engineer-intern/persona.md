# Software Engineer, Intern (2027) at Exa

## Role Summary

Exa hires a Software Engineer Intern onto a small, systems-heavy applied-AI-lab team in San Francisco (onsite, full-time; the JD asks the intern to be willing to take a semester off to work full-time in SF). The intern is handed load-bearing infrastructure problems: recreating Google-class keyword search over ~10 billion pages, building a crawler that works optimally for any website, and building a custom vector database that serves over a billion vectors in under 100ms. The JD's *requirements* are deliberately spare and generalist-technical — currently pursuing a degree in CS / Engineering / Physics or a related technical field, experience with a high-performance language (C++, Rust, or similar), a habit of coding technically difficult or interesting projects for fun, and genuine care about the problem of finding high-quality information. Company *identity* is web-scale search infrastructure: crawling, keyword + neural/semantic retrieval, embeddings, and an in-house billion-vector database engineered for sub-100ms latency.

## Track Decision

- **screen_track:** full-stack
- **differentiator:** search infrastructure / high-performance systems (custom vector database, web-scale crawling, low-latency retrieval, information-retrieval / embeddings depth)
- **track_divergence:** true

The JD's literal requirements are a generalist strong-engineer spec — a technical degree in progress plus fluency in a high-performance language and a demonstrated love of hard projects — so the resume spine must pass a general-SWE screen: genuinely engineered, shipped software with real users, backend services / APIs, data pipelines, and cloud/CI, with the JD's languages demonstrated in bullets rather than parked in a Skills list. Because Exa's engineering identity is performance-critical search infrastructure, the page must *also* register real high-performance-systems depth (a high-performance language used for something hard — memory discipline, concurrency, lock-free / low-allocation design, real-time or measured-latency constraints) AND information-retrieval fit (semantic / vector search, embeddings, crawling, ranking, measured retrieval latency) prominently in the top half. On a 7-second scan the resume should read "ships real engineered software," and a parallel read should register "builds hard high-performance systems and cares about retrieval." It must not collapse to either pole. Rust is JD-listed but candidate-absent; it is a legitimate out-of-rails gap to record honestly, never to fabricate — a high-performance language demonstrated in real systems work is the transferable proof.

## Team & Bar

The reader is a systems-literate engineer at a small applied-AI lab, screening a low-volume, high-bar funnel where a human reads the PDF early (`recruiting.md` Part I §5: startups / labs are resume-first, founder-direct and referral outreach move the needle; no high-volume OA gauntlet). The resume screen and the technical depth behind it are the binding filters. The bar is evidence of independently built, shipped software AND at least one entry with genuine high-performance-systems depth (a high-performance language driving memory / concurrency / latency engineering, or measured latency/throughput wins) AND some information-retrieval signal (semantic or vector search, embeddings, crawling, ranking). Polyglot breadth helps, but breadth with no deep, defensible systems bullet reads as generalist. "Codes technically difficult projects for fun" is a real screen signal here — self-directed, hard, from-scratch engineering is rewarded over resume-padding. Visa sponsorship is not a knockout (Exa sponsors); the semester-off willingness is an apply-time commitment, not something inferred from the page.

## Screen Criteria

- Shipped-software evidence through bullets: real applications / products with users or institutional delivery, backend services / APIs, data or ingestion pipelines, cloud deployment (AWS, Docker, CI) — not Skills-only keyword stuffing.
- High-performance-systems depth in the top half: at least one entry where a high-performance language (C++/Rust/similar) drives hard engineering — memory management, concurrency, lock-free / zero-allocation design, real-time constraints, or measured latency/throughput — the signal that makes a candidate memorable past a generalist screen.
- Information-retrieval / search fit: semantic or vector search, embeddings, crawling / scraping, ranking, or measured retrieval latency demonstrated in a bullet — the "finding high-quality information" and vector-DB alignment Exa hires for.
- JD languages demonstrated in bullets (a high-performance language in real project/experience context), not listed in Skills alone; the required high-performance language must be present and backed by a decision, not orphaned.
- Strong signals: self-built products with paying users or real institutional delivery, sole-engineer ownership, quantified impact metrics (latency, throughput, cost, scale), release-quality artifacts, from-scratch / for-fun hard projects, live GitHub.
- Anti-patterns: high-performance / systems keywords in Skills with no bullet decision behind them; single-bullet thin entries; club-ops or growth framing without engineering substance; a generalist spine that buries the systems / retrieval differentiator at the bottom of the page; notebook-only ML with no delivery; a required high-performance language claimed but never demonstrated.
- When the track diverges, first read says "ships real engineered software" while the top half also proves high-performance-systems engineering and retrieval fit — neither pole alone passes this screen.
