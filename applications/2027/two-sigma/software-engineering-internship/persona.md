# Software Engineering Internship (Summer 2027) at Two Sigma

## Role Summary

Ten-week Summer 2027 SWE intern at Two Sigma's Soho NYC office. Hired into a general Software Engineering role and matched to a team 2–3 months before start; one project, an assigned mentor, and a final presentation. JD surface work spans the firm's engineering surface: distributed storage and public/private cloud used by other teams; reliability of mission-critical products including the main trading system; open-source and proprietary environments that test and deploy quantitative strategies (models); low-latency high-performance trade-execution infrastructure; pipelines that ingest data from 10,000+ sources daily; and internal tools that raise productivity. Requirements are generalist high-bar SWE: coursework toward a technical or quantitative degree; experience with Java, C, C++, Python, Ruby, Perl, or other JVM-targeting languages; large-scale systems; exceptional programming; analytical, organizational, and interpersonal skill; a passion for building quality software. Financial experience is not required. Company identity is a quantitative investment manager where technology is the main advantage — scientific, collaborative, data-passionate, pragmatic.

## Track Decision

- **screen_track:** full-stack (general SWE — the JD requirements literally test Java/C/C++/Python/Ruby/Perl/JVM languages, large-scale systems, and exceptional programming)
- **differentiator:** high-performance trading + data-ingestion systems (fintech-backend / low-latency) — mission-critical trading reliability, low-latency execution, 10k-source ingest, distributed storage/cloud, model test/deploy
- **track_divergence:** true — generic-SWE requirements at a quant firm whose engineering identity is low-latency / high-scale trading and data-ingestion infrastructure, not a product-SWE or CRUD shop. Spine stays `full-stack` (`reference/recruiting.md` III §11, `reference/resume.md` III §12). Differentiator-aligned systems and ingest/pipeline evidence stay prominent and deep. The page does not become a quant-research or trading-desk resume.

The spine is general SWE: ship real, defensible software; strong DS&A for a Hard CodeSignal / coding loop; JD languages the candidate can actually interview in, proven in bullets. The differentiator is carried by whatever on-page work demonstrates high-performance systems (concurrency, memory discipline, latency/throughput, correctness under load) and large-scale data ingestion / pipelines — kept in the first-read window, not buried.

## Team & Bar

Two Sigma is S-tier quant (`reference/companies.md`) — strong engineering culture, Python/C++ core, 4–5 rounds, Hard CodeSignal, systems-design yes, bottleneck: resume, <1%. The screener is an engineer/recruiter at a firm that advertises Putnam / ACM ICPC / IMO / Unix Lifetime Achievement peers and treats exceptional programming plus large-scale systems as floors. Funnel: Avature apply → resume screen (published bottleneck) → CodeSignal OA (Hard; official interview page does not name the platform — invitation controls) → technical loop (official page: up to ~3 rounds; first block often three 60-min sessions covering DS&A, OOP, C/C++/Java/Python; possible systems-design) → later project/experience conversations. Comp is $3,800/week at Bachelor's. What wins the human screen: C++ and Python proven in real systems work, not Skills-only; large-scale or high-performance evidence (ingestion, distributed/cloud, concurrency, measured latency/throughput); honest language depth (do not invent Java/C/Ruby/Perl); shipped, defensible projects a candidate can walk decision-by-decision; curiosity and pragmatism without fake desk experience. Class-year on page must show a current student with coursework toward a technical or quantitative degree (Expected May 2028 qualifies; no class-year knockout on the posting).

## Screen Criteria

- C++ and Python demonstrated in **bullets**, not Skills alone — both are load-bearing in the JD language list and the firm's Notes/interview page. Java/C/Ruby/Perl are named on the JD; listing them without bullet proof is worse than omitting them.
- Large-scale systems or high-performance depth on the page: ingestion/pipelines, distributed or cloud-backed delivery, concurrency, memory discipline, reliability, measured latency/throughput — the differentiator that separates this firm from a generic SWE screen.
- Exceptional programming and design in real, shipped, defensible work — trade-offs the candidate can narrate; strong CS fundamentals visible for a Hard CodeSignal and a DS&A/OOP loop.
- End-to-end ownership and production impact with metrics (latency, throughput, %, scale) — soft, metric-free claims read thin at this bar.
- Analytical signal without finance-jargon padding: statistics/econ coursework or rigorously evaluated data systems are enough; prior finance is not required.
- DS&A / OOP foundation visible (coursework + engineered systems) for CodeSignal + three 60-min technical sessions; systems-design fluency is a plus, not a fake distributed-systems claim.
- Class-year on page shows a current student with coursework toward a technical or quantitative degree (Expected May 2028 is in window).

## Anti-Patterns

- Re-targeting to an ML-research / LoRA / agent-pipeline lead as if this were a quant-research internship — the req is general SWE; models here are quant strategies the infra tests and deploys.
- All-CRUD / all-web-app page with no systems-performance or ingestion/pipeline signal — reads generic at a firm whose bottleneck is the resume and whose identity is trading/data infra.
- Java, C, Ruby, or Perl named in Skills with zero bullet evidence.
- C++ or Python named in Skills with zero bullet evidence.
- Finance-jargon padding or an invented trading-desk internship — prior finance is not required, and faking it reads worse than honest CS + systems + data.
- Soft, metric-free shipping claims where correctness, scale, and performance are the firm's selling point.
- Thin single-bullet filler that dilutes a strong systems + ingest spine.
- Club-ops or community-growth framing with no engineering the role tests.
