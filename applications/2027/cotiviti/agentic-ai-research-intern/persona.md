# Intern – Agentic AI Research at Cotiviti

## Role Summary

A remote US paid internship ($32–40/hr) on Cotiviti's R&D side researching generative and agentic AI for healthcare informatics — improving LLM/ML tools, designing autonomous agents for healthcare workflows, maintaining an AI Governance Program (ethics/safety for agentic AI), and liaising with product, business units, academic partners, and vendors. The JD surface is applied AI/ML research and engineering: generative model development, vector databases, ML/deep-learning models, cloud-hosted data pipelines (AWS/Azure), and cross-functional communication — not a pure data-analyst or IT-support seat. The company identity is healthcare payment/quality analytics at scale; the memorable angle is agentic/generative AI applied to regulated healthcare operations with explicit governance expectations.

## Team & Bar

The reviewer is likely a small R&D / emerging-technology team hiring one or a few research-oriented interns, not a high-volume campus OA funnel. They reward candidates who can show real generative-AI and agentic-system construction (not API-wrapper demos), ML workflow literacy (training, validation, error analysis — or honest applied equivalents at intern scope), vector-database and embedding use in anger, cloud + large-dataset pipeline experience, and communication discipline for governance and cross-functional updates. Third-party validation (eval harnesses, held-out metrics, shipped systems) matters more than club membership or generic "interest in AI." Anti-patterns: skills-list LLM keywords with no bullet proof; notebook `model.fit()` with no pipeline or eval story; a resume that reads pure web/full-stack with AI bolted into Skills only; no signal of testing, governance, or safety-minded evaluation when the role explicitly owns AI Governance support.

## Screen Criteria

- End-to-end ML or generative-AI workflow visible on the page — preprocessing/feature or data ingestion → modeling or LLM adaptation → validation/eval → error analysis or governance gates — not isolated tool names.
- Agentic AI / LLM orchestration demonstrated through multi-step pipelines, agents, or eval frameworks; "LangGraph" or equivalent patterns in bullets beat a bare Skills mention.
- Vector-database or embedding retrieval literacy shown through real use (semantic search, RAG, stored embeddings), not listed only under Databases.
- Cloud (AWS and/or Azure) and ML data-pipeline experience evidenced in bullets — ETL at scale, containerized services, or production deployment.
- AI governance / safety / ethics reads as eval rigor, adversarial testing, consensus gates, or validation discipline — transferable to "AI Governance Program" work even without healthcare domain claims.
- Healthcare technology interest is a plus in the JD; absence of clinical domain is not disqualifying if ML/agentic depth is strong, but the page should not read as ignoring the healthcare mission entirely when other signals are equal.
- Anti-patterns: leading with unrelated lanes (pure audio DSP, fintech deal-sourcing without ML) that bury generative-AI substance; metric-free single-bullet entries for ML claims; Azure or TensorFlow named in Skills with zero bullet support.
