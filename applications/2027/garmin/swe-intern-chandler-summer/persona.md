# SOFTWARE ENGINEER INTERN at Garmin (Chandler, AZ)

## Role Summary

A full-time Summer 2027 Software Engineering Intern (iCIMS 19858) on-site at Garmin's Chandler, AZ office. The intern learns to develop software for Garmin communication and navigation products under supervision, with placement into one of Embedded Software, DevOps, Mobile, or Web. Essential functions are learn-to: C/C++/Assembly (or other selected languages) per Garmin methodology; test with debuggers, emulators, simulators, and logic analyzers; releases and QA; maintenance of in-production products plus new-product design; integrate and give feedback on products in development.

JD surface vs company identity: the hard screen is generic SWE — CS/EE/CE (or relevant), GPA ≥3.0, a high-level language (C, C++, C#, or Java), and data structures or OOP. Garmin's memorable identity is GPS/aviation/marine consumer hardware: Chandler is aviation engineering next to comms and navigation products, not a generic SaaS internship.

## Track Decision

- **screen_track:** full-stack (JD requirements literally test general SWE: C/C++/C#/Java, data structures, OOP — default general SWE per `reference/resume.md` Part III §12 and `reference/recruiting.md` Part III §11)
- **differentiator:** embedded / consumer-hardware / C++ systems / aviation (GPS + Chandler aviation comms/nav products)
- **track_divergence:** true

The literal screen is not ML frameworks/training (`resume.md` §14), not ROS/perception/controls (`resume.md` §15), and not a CI/CD-first SRE req (`resume.md` §13) even though DevOps is one placement lane. Mobile/Web are placement options, not the requirement bar. The resume spine is full-stack/general SWE. The company-fit signal is systems-depth C++ next to hardware, real-time or lock-free constraints, and disciplined test/debug — kept prominent and deep, not collapsed into a pure web page, an agentic-LLM product page, or a pretended firmware/assembly internship.

## Team & Bar

The reviewer is a B-tier GPS/avionics university recruiter (iCIMS; 2–3 rounds; Easy–Med practical; intern reports no standard OA; bottleneck: resume; ~8–15%). With no OA, the PDF is the gate (`recruiting.md` Part II §8; mid-size / non-tech-tech, resume-first). Recruiter voice: a current CS/EE/CE student with GPA ≥3.0 (3.5+ is a desired plus), who has already written software in a listed high-level language, can talk data structures or OOP, and would not be lost next to embedded test tools or a hardware-adjacent product. Assembly, debuggers, emulators, and logic analyzers are learn-on-the-job essential functions, not a filter that the intern must already own. Master's in Geomatics/Aerospace/Software Engineering is desired, not required. Prestige is a tiebreaker, not a gate. No class-year gate; JD prints no sponsorship knockout.

## Screen Criteria

- JD languages demonstrated *inside* architectural decisions — especially C++ in bullets, not Skills alone. C, C#, Java, and Assembly are named on the JD; do not invent them.
- Data structures or object-oriented design shown through real software (class modeling, encapsulation, ownership of a component), not a coursework list.
- Implement / test / debug evidence with sized outcomes (failure recovery, latency, real-time constraints, release discipline) — software quality and maintenance are first-class on this req.
- Full-stack spine still present: shipped services, data, deploy, ownership of something used by others (`resume.md` §12) so Embedded/DevOps/Mobile/Web routing stays plausible.
- Company-fit differentiator visible in the top half: real-time or systems C++, hardware-adjacent or lock-free/embedded-style constraints — without turning the page into a hobby-audio or aviation-only resume, and without fabricating firmware, ROS, or avionics internships.
- GPA on page supports the ≥3.0 floor and the ≥3.5 desired bar. Class-year on page shows current enrollment (`Expected May 2028` is a current student for a Summer 2027 intern cycle).
- Team-environment signal (desired qual) through titled collaboration or stakeholder delivery, not club-ops filler.

## Anti-Patterns

- Skills-list C++/Java/C with zero bullet proof — the floor signal at a Garmin SWE intern screen.
- Listing C, C#, Java, or Assembly that the page cannot defend.
- Leading with notebook ML or agentic-LLM product framing as if this were an applied-ML internship.
- A purely web resume with the systems/C++ signal buried or omitted at an aviation/comms hardware company.
- Claiming firmware, assembly, aviation internships, Garmin product work, or debugger/emulator lab experience that was never done.
- Club-ops or thin single-bullet filler that dilutes shipped engineering.
- Tutorial clones with no OOP/DS evidence, no debug story, and no shipped artifact.
