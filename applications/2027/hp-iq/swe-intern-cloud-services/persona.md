# Software Engineer Intern, Cloud Services (Summer 2027) at HP IQ

## Role Summary

A Summer 2027 paid internship in San Francisco on HP IQ's Cloud Services team — HP's AI innovation lab, not HP/HPE campus SWE. Interns develop, test, and operate integrated cloud services that support and extend HP IQ hardware, collaborating with hardware, AI, and platform engineering. The work includes projects around AI assistants, conference-room devices, and related device-cloud surfaces. The JD surface is a generic SWE intern req (independent execution, cross-functional communication, interest in cloud/hardware-software/AI) with Spring Boot as a preferred qualification, not a named language floor. HP IQ's company identity is the cloud foundation under AI-driven devices — services that make hardware products work as a system, not an ML-training lab and not a general corporate backend rotation.

## Track Decision

- **screen_track:** full-stack
- **differentiator:** device-cloud / AI-hardware ecosystem
- **track_divergence:** true

The literal requirements test general SWE / full-stack delivery on a cloud-services team (`recruiting.md` Part III §11; `resume.md` Part III §12): ship and operate services, talk to other teams, show interest in cloud, hardware-software integration, and AI. They do not test ML frameworks, training, or inference serving (`resume.md` §14 — that is the AML Platform sibling), and they do not name Kubernetes, Terraform, or SRE as the bar (`resume.md` §13). Spring Boot is preferred, not required. The resume spine is therefore full-stack / general SWE with real backend and cloud-service depth. HP IQ's memorable company-fit signal is software that extends devices — hardware-software integration, on-device or real-time adjacency, automation/infra next to services that scale. The resume leads with the full-stack cloud spine **and** keeps device-cloud / systems evidence prominent and deep in the top half. It does not collapse into a pure web CRUD page, a Spring-only Java resume, or an AML/fine-tuning/vLLM packet.

## Team & Bar

The reviewer is screening a B-tier intern cohort (`companies.md`: 2–3 rds · Easy · no standard OA · bottleneck: resume + degree-level knockout · ~10–15%) on Greenhouse. With no published OA, a human reads the PDF early (`recruiting.md` Part I §1, Part II §8). Recruiter voice: can this intern ship cloud services in a small, fast lab, talk to hardware and platform partners, and show real backend/deploy work rather than club-ops or notebook ML? Signals that win: backend languages and frameworks demonstrated inside bullets (Python, TypeScript, C++ if present — Spring Boot is preferred and absence is not a skills-line invention); shipped APIs, cloud deploy, containers, automation/CI; evidence of services at scale (queues, workers, throughput, failure recovery); hardware-software or on-device/real-time adjacency visible without claiming HP device firmware. Impact metrics that size latency, cost, reliability, or delivery beat vanity counts.

**Degree-level bar (abstract):** the posting lists currently pursuing a Master's or PhD as an essential qualification. An undergraduate degree line on the page does not satisfy that filter. The form also asks whether the candidate graduates before September 2027. Screen the PDF as if the knockout may already have fired; do not treat a strong undergrad page as having cleared eligibility.

## Screen Criteria

- Shows shipped cloud/backend services: REST (or equivalent) APIs, a data store, and deploy/cloud — at least one product defensible layer-by-layer (`resume.md` §12).
- Demonstrates programming languages through use in bullets, not Skills alone. Spring Boot is preferred; do not treat a missing Java/Spring line as a fabrication-to-fill, and do not treat Skills-only Spring as proof.
- Shows automation and infrastructure through use (containers, CI, cloud hosts, workers/queues) — not "familiar with Docker" with no demonstrated use.
- Shows exposure to building services at scale (throughput, workers, failure recovery, cost/latency), not a single tutorial endpoint.
- Keeps the device-cloud differentiator visible in the top half: hardware-software integration, on-device or real-time/systems discipline, AI used to extend a product — without turning the page into AML-platform (fine-tuning, vLLM, PyTorch training) or a hobby-audio-only resume.
- Uses impact metrics (latency, cost, failure recovery, scale, delivery) at roughly one metric per two bullets (`resume.md` §4).
- Education on the page is read as stated. Do not infer a Master's from coursework or title inflation.

Anti-patterns: leading with LoRA / PyTorch / vLLM / model-training as if this were the AML Platform intern; inventing Spring Boot, Java, Snowflake, Databricks, Copilot, Fusion, or Tableau; a purely frontend page with no cloud/services; club-ops without engineering depth; notebook ML without delivery; claiming a graduate degree the document does not state.
