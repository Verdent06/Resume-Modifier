Two kinds of work I already do map onto this intern seat: shipping services a non-engineer uses, and debugging a production failure until the number moves.

As a Michigan Data Consulting extracurricular (campus client project — not a second job on this form) I was the only engineer on a five-month contract for Michigan Campaign Finance Network. I replaced ~2-hour manual committee research with a Requests + Pandas ETL and shipped a Flask REST API on AWS EC2 into their public research workflow, eliminating ~800 hours of pulls across 400 PACs. That is the closest analog I have to "features and services" used by someone who is not me.

On Vylet I diagnosed a name-collision bug that was rejecting valid leads because same-name is not same-entity. I rebuilt the check as a triangulated 0–100 Python gate (no LLM): fuzzy-match the query, the state business registry, and a live site crawl, then hard-fail on legal status, industry, geography, or independence. Qualification went from 79% to 89% with no change in sourcing volume. At CaseStudyPrep.AI I owned a 27% S3 upload-failure rate — expired presigned URLs and MIME types Angular silently rejected — and moved audio off the main thread into a Web Worker.

I have not taught a language course, shipped lesson content, or run Duolingo's experiment stack. What I can bring on day one is Python services, tests/CI, and a habit of measuring whether a change actually worked. CS + Economics is why I care that a ship has a number attached — not a claim that I have already run learner A/B tests.
