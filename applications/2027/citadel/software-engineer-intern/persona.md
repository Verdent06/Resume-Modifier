# Software Engineer – Intern (US) — Summer 2027 at Citadel

## Role Summary

SWE intern on a small Citadel (hedge fund) engineering team — not Citadel Securities. The job is to turn investment ideas into production software: tools that bring trading strategies to life, high-performance large-data research platforms, system architectures, platforms, and web frameworks. Short development cycles mean work reaches production fast. The JD surface is generalist, high-bar SWE — "exceptional programming and design skills" — layered over the firm’s identity as an elite multi-strategy investment manager that applies distributed computing, NLP, and machine learning to markets. Two signals sit alongside the general-SWE spine: (1) high-performance systems fluency (research platforms, not HFT market-making), and (2) familiarity with probability and statistics. This is a general-SWE internship at a hedge fund, not an ML-research role and not a trading-desk internship — treat the AI/quant language as domain flavor and analytical-bar signal, not a pivot to notebook ML or fabricated finance experience.

## Track Decision

- **screen_track:** full-stack (general SWE — the JD requirements literally test programming, design, system architecture, platform and web-framework building)
- **differentiator:** hedge-fund / investment-tech identity — high-performance large-data research platforms **and** quantitative/statistical rigor (probability, statistics, model evaluation) **and** production platform/web-framework shipping. C++ and Python are the firm’s published core intern languages even though this posting body does not name them.
- **track_divergence:** false — the differentiator is flavor on a general-SWE spine, not a second track. The screen still reads as general SWE (`reference/recruiting.md` III §11, `reference/resume.md` III §12); the research-platform + stats + systems signal is what makes the candidate memorable here, not a re-target to ai-ml.

The spine is general SWE: ship real, defensible software; strong DS&A/CS fundamentals for a hard OA; breadth across the stack with depth in at least one layer. The differentiator is carried by whatever on-page work demonstrates high-performance systems engineering, quantitative/statistical modeling on a research/data platform, and production APIs/platforms — kept prominent and deep, but the resume does not become an ML-research page or a fake trading-desk page.

## Team & Bar

Citadel is S-tier quant/hedge fund (`reference/companies.md`) — one of the most selective SWE intern funnels in the industry (<1% acceptance; hard HackerRank OA; math-and-systems-first technical loop). The screener is an engineer/recruiter at a firm where programming excellence and analytical rigor are both floors, not extras. Funnel: application (150–300 word personal-story essay + technical links) → resume screen → HackerRank OA → multiple technical rounds → behavioral/fit. Comp is top-of-market ($4,500–$5,800/week). What wins the human screen: exceptional programming and design proven in real projects the candidate can defend decision-by-decision; systems/performance depth that reads investment-tech-adjacent; demonstrated comfort with probability/statistics (regression, model evaluation, quantitative analysis) rather than surface ML; production platform/API shipping. Prior finance is not required; a trading-desk internship is not required; intellectual curiosity and hard-problem passion are. Class-year on page must show a current student returning to school (Expected May 2028 qualifies).

## Screen Criteria

- Exceptional programming and design demonstrated in **real, shipped, defensible** work — the candidate can narrate every trade-off; strong CS fundamentals visible for a hard DS&A OA.
- **C++ and Python** demonstrated in bullets, not Skills alone — they are the firm’s published core intern languages and the load-bearing pair for this screen.
- High-performance / real-time **systems** depth surfaced somewhere on the page: concurrency, memory-discipline, latency/throughput numbers — investment-tech adjacent, not a claim of exchange matching-engine work.
- **Quantitative / statistical** rigor demonstrated: probability, statistics, regression, or rigorous model evaluation on a data/research platform — maps to "familiarity with probability and statistics" and "high-performance, large data research platforms." A statistically validated research/data system beats notebook toy ML.
- Production **platforms / web frameworks / APIs** with metrics (latency, throughput, %, scale) — the JD names system architecture, platforms, and web frameworks as the intern’s actual work.
- Breadth across the stack with real depth in at least one layer; a live GitHub backing the claims.
- DS&A / OOP foundation and analytical coursework (statistics, discrete math, probability) visible — the analytical bar is explicit in the JD.
- Class-year on page shows a current student returning to school after the internship (Expected May 2028 is in window).

## Anti-Patterns

- Re-targeting to an ML-research / LoRA / agent-pipeline lead as if this were an AI-research internship because the JD names ML/NLP — it is a general-SWE role at a hedge fund.
- Claiming or implying a **trading-desk internship** or market-making / Citadel Securities identity the candidate does not have — this posting is the hedge fund, and prior finance is not required.
- All-CRUD / all-web-app page with no systems-performance or quantitative signal — reads generic and off-differentiator at this bar.
- Soft, metric-free shipping claims where latency/throughput/scale numbers are expected at a performance-obsessed firm.
- Finance-jargon padding with no engineering substance (faking a trading background reads worse than honest strong CS).
- Thin single-bullet filler entries that dilute a strong systems + quantitative + production spine.
- Statistics/probability claimed only in a skills list with nothing on the page that actually exercises it.
- C++ or Python named in Skills with zero bullet evidence.
