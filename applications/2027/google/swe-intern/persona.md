# Software Engineering Intern, BS, Summer 2027 at Google

## Role Summary

A paid 12–14 week US-based Summer 2027 software engineering internship (BS track — not the MS intern req, not new grad, not STEP). Comp is US $98k–$131k + 0% bonus. The intern joins a specific project: design, test, deploy, and maintain software that ships at Google product scale (Ads, Chrome, Android, YouTube, etc.). Recruiting may route SWE vs SRE from the resume. JD surface is generic intern SWE: two or more general-purpose languages, data structures or algorithms, Bachelor's-track enrollment. Company identity is large-scale distributed product engineering and host-match versatility — not a single product internship and not an ML-research seat.

## Track Decision

- **screen_track:** full-stack (general SWE)
- **differentiator:** large-scale / distributed systems / Google-product engineering (host-match versatility across infra, web, AI/ML-adjacent shipping — not a single product)
- **track_divergence:** true

The literal requirements test current Bachelor's-track status, coding in two or more general-purpose languages (C, C++, Java, JavaScript, Python named), and DS&A from school or work. That is a general SWE screen (`recruiting.md` Part III §11; `resume.md` Part III §12), so the resume spine is full-stack: shipped applications, APIs, data systems, Python/JS-family delivery, deploy/containers where real. They do not name Kubernetes, ROS, or model-training frameworks as a hard floor, so the spine is not `dev-ops`, `robotics`, or `ai-ml`. The company-fit signal is software that belongs next to Google-scale systems — distributed or parallel work, production services, C++/systems discipline, web or data pipelines a host can match — without fabricating Java, Go, mobile, or internal Google infra. Lead with the full-stack spine and keep distributed / systems / large-software evidence prominent and deep.

## Team & Bar

The reviewer is an S-tier university recruiter plus, later, a host matching an intern onto one project (`companies.md`: 2–3 rds · Easy–Med · Google Docs · no intern sys design · bottleneck: host match · ~4–5%). Recruiter voice: “can this junior who returns to school write, debug, and ship in more than one language, and could a team imagine them on a real Google project?” — not “did they intern at Google already.” What wins on paper: listed languages shown inside bullets, not Skills alone; a shipped application or pipeline with ownership and impact metrics; enough distributed/systems depth to be memorable at host match. Prestige is a tiebreaker (`recruiting.md` §8). After the PDF, Google Docs DS&A is the coding filter; host match is the conversion filter. Behavioral is a filter round (`recruiting.md` §6). Preferred: US degree program, 12 weeks outside term, returning to school — class-year on the page must make that obvious.

## Screen Criteria

- JD languages demonstrated in bullets, not Skills alone — especially Python and C++, plus a JavaScript-family language through actual product surfaces. Java, Go, C, C#, mobile are named as examples; absence is not a fabrication invitation.
- Shipped full-stack or backend systems: APIs, data stores, client surfaces, deployment, operational ownership — not tutorial CRUD or club-ops without engineering (`resume.md` §12).
- Differentiator visible in the top half: distributed or parallel systems, production services, large-software or infra-adjacent delivery — without burying the full-stack spine or converting the page to notebook ML or hobby DSP-only.
- DS&A signal on the page (coursework and/or algorithmic/systems decisions in bullets) — a named minimum qualification.
- Impact metrics (latency, cost, failure recovery, scale, hours saved) rather than vanity counts.
- Class-year on page: current Bachelor's enrollment with a return-to-school date after Summer 2027.

## Anti-Patterns

- Skills-list Python/C++/JavaScript-family with zero bullet evidence at a company where polyglot-in-use is a floor.
- Leading with LoRA / agentic-GTM product framing as if this were an ML or founder internship.
- Club-ops, GTM/quant sourcing, or deal-flow work with no engineering the role tests.
- Distributed systems / Linux / infrastructure named only in Skills.
- Frontend-only polish or notebook ML with no pipeline, deploy, or systems story.
- Thin single-bullet filler that dilutes a shippable + systems spine.
- Invented Java, Go, Android, Chrome, Ads, SRE-oncall, or Google-internal stack the page cannot defend.
