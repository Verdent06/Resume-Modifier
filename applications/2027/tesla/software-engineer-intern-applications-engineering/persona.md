# Software Engineer Intern, Applications Engineering (Winter/Spring 2027) at Tesla

## Role Summary

A Winter/Spring 2027, full-time (40 hours/week), on-site Fremont internship. The application is considered across two sibling teams: Applications Engineering (architect, build, manage, and operate infrastructure and applications for Tesla Engineering — Connected Systems, Firmware, and Autopilot) and Business Ops & Infrastructure (services and tools that power internal software and processes). The intern plans, designs, implements, and runs ongoing infrastructure components that support vehicle and technology development. The JD surface is general SWE plus infra/services: enrolled CS/IT student graduating 2027–2028; proficiency in one or more of Python, Go, Java, C/C++; experience building or maintaining infrastructure and services; CS fundamentals and well-organized code; cross-functional collaboration with independent execution. Nice-to-haves are React, JavaScript, Angular, CSS, and SQL dialects (MySQL, MS SQL, Oracle). Tesla's company identity is EV / vehicle engineering at production scale — this req is the *support-software* layer for those teams, not Autopilot model training, vehicle firmware, chip EDA, or Energy Commercial UI.

## Track Decision

- **screen_track:** full-stack
- **differentiator:** EV / vehicle-engineering internal tools & infrastructure
- **track_divergence:** true

The literal requirements test general SWE plus infrastructure-and-services delivery, with optional web (React/JS/Angular) — not ML training/inference, not ROS/autonomy, not SRE-only platform engineering (`recruiting.md` Part III §11; `resume.md` Part III §12). The resume spine is therefore full-stack / software-generalist: APIs and services, web where evidenced, data stores, cloud/deploy. Tesla's memorable company-fit signal is internal tools and infrastructure that power how vehicles and Autopilot/Firmware teams actually develop — production-style ownership next to real products, not a consumer CRUD app and not a claim of having written FSD or firmware. The resume leads with the full-stack + infra/services spine **and** keeps vehicle-engineering-adjacent systems discipline (how products are designed, built, and operated; C++/Python depth; deployed internal tools) prominent — it does not flip into Autopilot-ML, firmware-only, or frontend-only.

## Team & Bar

The reviewer is screening a B-tier Tesla intern cohort (`companies.md`: 3 rds · Medium · HackerRank · no intern system design · bottleneck: tech rounds · ~5–8%) before an OA-gated coding loop. Recruiter voice: can this intern ship and operate internal applications and infrastructure that other engineering teams depend on, in a fast, execution-heavy culture? Strong signals: JD-floor languages demonstrated inside bullets (Python and/or C++ — Go/Java are named but absence is not a knockout if the page proves polyglot services depth another way); evidence of building and maintaining infrastructure and services (APIs, containers, cloud deploy, internal tools); software-generalist or full-stack breadth (web + backend + data); metrics that size real outcomes (latency, cost, failure recovery, scale, delivery); curiosity about how products are designed, built, and operated without fabricating vehicle-domain work. Prestige is a tiebreaker, not a gate (`recruiting.md` §8). The OA is the high-elimination stage at this volume (`recruiting.md` §1, §8); the resume's job is to win team-match for Applications Engineering vs the wrong Tesla intern packet.

## Screen Criteria

- Demonstrates at least one JD-floor language through use in bullets — Python and/or C++ — not only in the Skills line. Go, Java, Oracle, and MS SQL named on the posting are not a screen fail if unevidenced; claiming them without bullet proof is.
- Shows building or maintaining infrastructure and services: APIs, deployment/cloud, containers, internal tools, or operated components — not "familiar with Docker" with no demonstrated use (`resume.md` §13 trap, applied as infra-adjacent evidence on a full-stack spine).
- Shows software-generalist / full-stack delivery: a web/UI layer, backend services, and data/storage, with at least one product defensible end to end (`resume.md` §12).
- Keeps EV / vehicle-engineering support-software identity visible in the top half (internal tools, production infra, systems discipline, curiosity about how products operate) without turning the page into Autopilot-ML, firmware, or chip-EDA.
- Uses impact metrics (latency, throughput, cost, failure recovery, scale, delivery) rather than vanity counts; roughly one metric per two bullets (`resume.md` §4).
- Anti-patterns: claiming Go, Java, Oracle, or MS SQL with no evidenced use; Autopilot-ML or vehicle-firmware framing; notebook ML without delivery; club-ops without engineering depth; frontend-only with no infrastructure or services; a consumer-SaaS page that never shows operated internal tools at an EV engineering-platform company.
