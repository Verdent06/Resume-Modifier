# Software Engineer, Intern (Summer 2027) at Aquatic Capital Management

## Role Summary

A Summer 2027, onsite Chicago software-engineering internship at an early-stage systematic hedge fund. The intern joins a fast-moving team building high-performance, distributed systems that power research and trading infrastructure — designing tools, improving system reliability, and optimizing the platform for low-latency, high-throughput data processing alongside engineers and quants. The JD surface is general/backend software engineering (Python and/or C++, algorithms, systems, computer architecture); the company identity is quant fintech, where the platform's correctness, latency, and throughput directly move live trading and research. Math-competition background and prior hedge-fund/prop-trading internships are explicit positive signals on the application.

## Track Decision

- **screen_track:** full-stack (general / backend-systems SWE)
- **differentiator:** low-latency, high-throughput distributed systems (quant fintech backend)
- **track_divergence:** false

The screen spine is general/backend SWE because the requirements literally test Python and/or C++, strong algorithms, systems, and computer-architecture fundamentals, plus refactoring and TDD — not ML research, ROS/autonomy, or CI/CD platform ownership. The differentiator (low-latency distributed systems, concurrency, throughput) lives inside the same backend-systems family as the screen track, so there is no divergent second track to protect; the strongest backend/systems evidence is both the spine and the company-fit signal.

## Team & Bar

The reviewer screens at an S-tier-quant bar: this is a Citadel-pedigree, ML-driven systematic fund where the platform is the product and reliability/latency are non-negotiable. Interns are expected to work directly on live-adjacent systems with senior engineers and quants. The bar is not full-stack breadth or club-ops; it is evidence that the candidate reasons like a systems engineer — concurrency, async orchestration, queueing, delivery guarantees, performance and latency measured with real numbers — and is genuinely curious about how things work. CS fundamentals are assumed (the OA and tech screens enforce them); the resume wins the screen and team-match, not the algorithm gate. C++ surface, distributed-systems depth, and shipped reliable systems carry disproportionate weight.

## Screen Criteria

- Demonstrates Python (and, where genuine, C++) through real systems work, not only in the skills line.
- Shows distributed / low-latency / high-throughput systems thinking: concurrency, async pipelines, queues, event buses, delivery guarantees, caching, or measured latency/throughput wins.
- Surfaces computer-architecture and algorithms fundamentals through engineering decisions, not as bare coursework.
- Leads with shipped, reliable systems whose trade-offs survive a deep technical dive (why this datastore, why this concurrency model, what broke and how it recovered).
- Uses impact metrics — latency, throughput, scale, recovery — so "high-performance" reads as measured engineering, not aspiration.
- Anti-patterns: tutorial-grade frontend or CRUD with no performance story; notebook ML with no delivery; club-membership padding without engineering depth; keyword-listed C++ with no bullet backing it.
