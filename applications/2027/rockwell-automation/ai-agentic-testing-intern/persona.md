# 2027 AI Center of Excellence Intern (Agentic AI for PLC Testing) at Rockwell Automation

## Role Summary

This is a research internship inside Rockwell's AI Center of Excellence, reporting to the Director of AI. The work is building agentic AI systems (LLM-driven) that read PLC code and control requirements, then automatically generate test plans, fault-injection scenarios, and validation runs executed in simulation — going beyond standard functional testing to surface corner cases, safety risks, and unspecified failure modes. The JD surface is squarely AI/ML research (LLMs, agentic workflows, formal verification, reasoning) applied to an industrial-automation/safety-critical domain (PLC programming, digital twins, control systems). This is not a product SWE internship — it is closer to an applied-research seat that happens to sit at the intersection of GenAI and industrial controls.

## Track Decision

- **screen_track:** ai-ml (applied/research sub-bucket — agentic systems and LLM workflows, not classical model-fitting)
- **differentiator:** industrial automation / safety-critical control systems — PLC programming, formal verification, simulation and digital twins
- **track_divergence:** true

The screen spine is AI/ML because every responsibility bullet is an agentic-AI or LLM research task (build agents, generate test plans, reason about faults, explore formal verification). The differentiator is the industrial/safety-critical domain the agents operate on — PLC logic, control systems, fault injection, compliance with operational constraints. A candidate with strong LLM/agentic depth but zero systems-rigor, testing discipline, or safety/fault-reasoning signal reads as a generic GenAI resume mismatched to a systems-safety research role.

## Team & Bar

The reviewer is a small, technical research team (reporting line: Director of AI CoE), not a high-volume campus recruiting funnel. There is no stated hard eligibility gate beyond active enrollment, so the bar is almost entirely resume content, not a keyword/eligibility filter. Given the explicit research framing (open, reproducible research; formal verification; safety-critical reasoning), the bar rewards evidence the candidate can build and evaluate real agentic/LLM systems with rigor — eval harnesses, fault/edge-case reasoning, systems-level or simulation exposure — over polished product-shipping metrics alone. A resume that reads as pure web/full-stack with an AI veneer, or pure classical ML with no agentic/LLM systems evidence, misses the actual research ask.

## Screen Criteria

- Demonstrates hands-on agentic AI / LLM workflow construction (orchestration, multi-step pipelines), not just calling an LLM API.
- Shows evaluation/testing rigor somewhere on the page — eval harnesses, adversarial test-case design, error analysis, or QA/validation discipline — the closest analog to "generating test plans and validation."
- Any signal of reasoning about faults, edge cases, safety constraints, or fault-tolerant system design (even outside industrial automation) reads as directly transferable to fault-injection/corner-case testing.
- Simulation, digital-twin, real-time systems, or embedded/hardware-adjacent engineering is a strong differentiator bonus given the PLC/simulation focus, but its absence is not disqualifying for an otherwise strong agentic-AI candidate.
- Math/CS fundamentals (algorithms, formal reasoning) support the "formal verification" research area without needing a dedicated formal-methods background.
- Anti-pattern: an AI/ML resume that is all notebook-style model training with no agentic/production system, or a resume that leans so hard into unrelated fields (audio DSP, fintech scoring) that no research-relevant AI system is visible in the top half.
