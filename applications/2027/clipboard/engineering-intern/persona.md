# Engineering Intern at Clipboard

## Role Summary

Clipboard is hiring an Engineering Intern to build and ship product features that directly affect how healthcare professionals and facilities connect. The JD surface is a general full-stack/product-engineering internship: embedded in an engineering team, working with experienced engineers, product managers, and designers, and expected to tackle meaningful user-facing problems from day one. The company identity is marketplace execution in a remote-first, written, high-autonomy culture.

## Track Decision

- **screen_track:** full-stack
- **differentiator:** marketplace-product
- **track_divergence:** false

The resume should read as a full-stack intern page with shipped systems, database/API decisions, real user or operational impact, and enough client-side/product surface to support a product-engineering screen. Marketplace-product fit is a specialization inside the full-stack screen, not a separate track.

## Team & Bar

The reader is likely screening for an intern who can contribute to a fast-moving product team without needing a toy project. The bar is evidence of building usable software across frontend, backend, data, and deployment surfaces, with decisions tied to user or workflow impact. Because Clipboard is a startup-style marketplace rather than an OA-first big-tech funnel, the PDF needs to pass a human skim early: a strong first read should say "this person ships product systems that real users depend on."

## Screen Criteria

- Shipped product or platform work should lead the page; research-only or robotics-only framing is off-axis for this role.
- Full-stack evidence should appear through bullets, not only Skills: frontend/client work, backend/API ownership, databases, auth, deployment, and operational reliability.
- Strong signals are real users, workflow automation, marketplace-like coordination, database trade-offs, and product features with measurable latency, scale, or process impact.
- Anti-patterns are notebook/research framing, club-growth narrative without software depth, and technology lists that do not show why the tool was chosen.
- The page should support practical interview probes on web development, APIs, data modeling, async/background work, and product trade-offs.
