## Screen Criteria

**Pass signals (abstract — the writer discovers which entries carry them):**
- Python demonstrated through use in bullets, not only the Skills line (`resume.md` keyword-through-use). SQL through-use is a plus (data analytics implied); not a named JD language.
- End-to-end data workflow: ingest messy or multi-source data → ETL / normalize / aggregate / score → API, database, or report → measured outcome. Analog to quality-system digital solutions and process improvement without claiming QMS/FDA tools.
- Advanced analytics that a stakeholder uses: rankings, precision/shortlist rates, eval lifts, PAC/entity scoring — analog to "customer-centric solutions for data analytics."
- Software development **and** a validation/SDLC analog: shipped services plus deterministic gates, held-out eval, or real-time safety discipline. Do not invent medical-device V&V internships.
- Collaboration with non-engineering users (researchers, principals, quality-adjacent stakeholders) — analog to Quality management / quality engineers.
- Statistics visible: coursework (Intro to Statistics) plus methods that are not a black box.
- Excel through messy-spreadsheet ingestion when the work is real. Microsoft Office is a required familiarity, not a Skills-line dump.

**Anti-patterns:**
- Invented Snowflake, Databricks, Tableau, Power BI, Copilot, Fusion, R, FDA 21 CFR, eQMS, or medical-device / hospital internships (`resume.md` §8 fabrication smell).
- Generic full-stack product resume with no ETL, analytics, or scoring.
- Notebook ML / `model.fit()` with no delivery, no pipeline, no measured outcome.
- Skills-only Python/data analytics with no bullet proof.
- Club-ops or community-growth filler occupying prime slots.
- Pure audio-DSP / embedded C++ as the **first-pass identity** with no data/analytics analog — C++ safety/validation analog may sit **near** the spine, not replace it.
- Quality-engineer title inflation or "prior quality related experience" claims the page cannot defend.
- Vanity metrics (uptime, LOC, coverage %) standing in for impact.
